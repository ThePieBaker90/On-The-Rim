## General Description
Most targets, be they creatures, vehicles, constructs, or something else, have some level of armour protection. Most weapons deal piercing or slashing damage, which this armour is designed to protect against. Depending on the armour adn weapon used, the armour may provide varying or no amount of protection. 

## Mechanics
- Every creature, vehicle, construct, etc. that can be targeted has an Armour Value (AV). This AV is one component of the target's overall hit class, alongside the Evade Value (EV). This value is on a scale from 0 to 50.
- Any source of piercing or slashing damage has an Armour Penetration (AP) value. This value also on a scale from 0 to 50. 
- When making an attack having AP against a target with an AV, subtract the AP from the AV to produce the effective armour protection value (EAPV).
  - This EAPV has a minimum value of 0. If AP>AV, the armour is overmatched and the EAPV is 0.
- Adding the EAPV and the EV, along with appropriate modifiers, results in the hit class of the target, and the difficulty class of the hit roll.
- Some particularly large targets, such as vehicles, may have multiple AVs.
  - For example, different AVs for the front, side, and rear of the vehicle.
  - Or, different AVs for different body parts of a very large bug.
- Worn armour should have its AV specified.
- Creatures, constructs, vehicles, etc. with integral armour should have the AV specified.
- AVs add when multiple are applicable. For example a thick-skinned bug with a bulletproof vest or something.
- Weapons should have their AP specified.
- Ammunition can have AP modifiers, both additive and subtractive, that combine with the weapon's AP. A common specialty ammunition type is armour piercing ammo, which will typically add penetration equivalent to roughly 25% the typical AP of the typical weapons that use that cartridge.
- For melee weapons, AP will typically be some value +Str
