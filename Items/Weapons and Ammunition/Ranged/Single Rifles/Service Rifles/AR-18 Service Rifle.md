## General Description
The Armstek Rifle 18 is a robust and reliable service rifle. Its design is centered around simplicity and reliability to minimize operator workload. With a comparable cost to the M299 Battle Rifle and AR-25B Marksman Rifle but lower performance than either, the AR-18 is not especially popular for professional military forces. However, its simplicity is valued for many secondary forces and corporate militias where heavy firepower is not as necessary and operator training is less extensive.

## Lore
An internal Armstek study conducted in 5411 determined that many operators of Armstek products were poorly-trained and largely disposable company enforcers. These enforcers were insufficiently trained to operate and maintain their rifles, resulting in poor reviews of these products. A proposed solution was to make a "dummy-proof" rifle with as simple operation and maintenance as possible. Development was extremely protracted as Armstek struggled to design this new class of weapon. Finally in 5417 the Armstek Rifle 18 was unveiled. Howard-Kasseler promptly sued for stolen technology, citing the AR-18's striking resemblance to their own Type 90 marksman rifle as well as a 5416 break-in at a HK archives facility. However, insufficient evidence was found to prove that Armstek actually stole HK technology, and the suit was disposed by Imperial mediators in 5421.
