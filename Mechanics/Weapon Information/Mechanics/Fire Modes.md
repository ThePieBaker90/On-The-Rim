## Description
A weapon, particularly a firearm, may have multiple fire modes. These change how the weapon's attack works. Unless otherwise specified, switching between fire modes is a free action.
Firearms will usually have the safe fire mode and one or two attacking fire modes. For example, it's common for assault rifles to have safe, semi-auto, and a typically 3-round burst. Machine guns will commonly have safe, automatic, and suppressive fire modes. Weapons such as sniper rifles or shotguns may only have manual action fire modes.

## Common Examples
This is not an exhaustive list of fire modes. If a weapon has a fire mode not on this list it should be described within the weapon's page. 
### Single-Shot Fire Modes
Some fire modes expend a single ammunition to fire a single round and make a single attack. Weapons that primarily use these modes are typically more powerful per shot with 2dX damage per round. 
- Semiautomatic: One ammunition is fired per attack action. The weapon automatically loads the next round into the firing position- no additional use restrictions.
- Manual Action: One ammunition is fired per attack action. Unless othewrise specified, a bonus action and two hands are required to chamber the next ammunition before the weapon can be fired again. A descriptor of the manual action in question (e.g. bolt, pump, lever) should be included.
### Multi-Shot Fire Modes
Some fire modes expend multiple ammunition to fire multiple rounds and make multiple attacks. However, only one attack roll is made which is used for every attack within this attack action. The roll result is reduced by the [Recoil](/Mechanics/Weapon%20Information/Recoil.md) value for each subsequent attack after the first in this attack action. Weapons that primarily use multi-shot fire modes typically are less powerful with 1dX damage per round.
- Burst: A fixed amount of ammunition, described on the weapon's page (e.g. Burst:3), is fired per attack action. The weapon automatically loads the next round into the firing position- no additional use restrictions. If the weapon has less ammunition loaded (e.g. 2 rounds) than the burst would typically expend (e.g. 3), the burst fire only has that many attacks (2). 
- Automatic: A die or dice roll amount of ammunition is fired per attack action, described on the weapon's page (e.g. 1d4), and this number may be modified by up to the shooter's firearm skill modifier (e.g. 1D4 +-2). The weapon automatically loads the next round into the firing position- no additional use restrictions. If the weapon has less ammunition loaded (e.g. 2 rounds) than the automatic fire would typically expend (e.g. 3), it only has that many attacks (2).
### Special Fire Modes
- Suppressive Fire: See [Suppressive Fire](/Mechanics/Weapon%20Information/Suppressive%20Fire.md). Used to suppress an area, not primarily deal damage.
- Area Attack: See [Area Attack](/Mechanics/Weapon%20Information/Area%20Attack.md). Rather than attacking a specific target, an area is attacked and all potential targets within that area make a save to avoid being hit.
- Moving Fire: Move and shoot rather than stopping to aim. The user moves up to half their movement speed and then can make an attack with any of their weapon's non-special fire modes, with one level of [Hipfire Penalty](Mechanics/Weapon%20Information/Hipfire%20Penalty.md) added. 
- Safe: The weapon cannot be fired in Safe mode.
